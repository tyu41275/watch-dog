## Questions

1. Who is the primary user for the three-day MVP, and what exact moment should feel meaningfully safer or easier?
2. What is the single must-win demo journey from opening the page to seeing a useful result?
3. In the MVP, should Watch Dog only explain/check a URL, accept community submissions, expose a browsable corpus, or combine a very small subset of those actions?
4. What role must WebMCP visibly play in the demo, and what would count as a convincing WebMCP interaction to the judges?
5. Which threat verdicts are acceptable for the MVP (for example: suspicious, likely safe, unknown), and what evidence must accompany them?
6. What data sources are allowed during the demo, and may any submitted URLs or metadata be retained?
7. Who may contribute to or moderate the community corpus, and what is the minimum abuse/spam/privacy safeguard required for submission?
8. What must the product never claim or do—for example, guarantee safety, automatically visit a live URL, or publish sensitive submissions?
9. Which environment and deployment constraints are fixed, including preferred language/framework, hosting, API keys, budget, and browser target?
10. What judging criteria, submission deadline, required assets, and team availability define the actual delivery window?
11. What three observable outcomes would make the MVP and the under-three-minute video successful?
12. Which attractive capabilities should be explicitly deferred to protect the less-than-three-day scope?
